The world has warmed, and not evenly in time

Every number on this page comes from one public file: NASA's annual global temperature record, 1880 to 2025. Scroll at your own pace. The chart and the text stay in step, and nothing needs a click.

The cool start

No year from 1880 to 1910 is above zero. The average for those 31 years is −0.26 °C.

The first climb

The anomaly rose from −0.44 °C in 1910 to +0.12 °C in 1940.

The flat middle

From 1940 to 1975 the yearly values swing around zero. Their average is −0.01 °C. Scientists attribute this stretch to a mix of aerosol pollution and natural variation; the temperature record alone cannot separate the two.

The steady rise

The anomaly went from −0.10 °C in 1976 to +0.39 °C in 2000.

The record decades

The average for 2001 to 2025 is +0.80 °C. Five years are above +1.0 °C: 2016, 2020, 2023, 2024 and 2025. The warmest year in the record is 2024, at +1.28 °C.

Every year as one stripe

Blue is cooler than the 1951 to 1980 average, red is warmer. The strip moves as you scroll through the years.

Where the line stops

The line ends at +1.18 °C in 2025. The 2026 row in the source is only partly filled in, so it is left out. Each value is an annual mean of monthly values, relative to the 1951 to 1980 average.
